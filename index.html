Research leadership  /  Enterprise AI  /  Austin, Texas

I run research for products that are hard to explain and easy to get wrong.

Twenty years across hardware, software, and the ecosystems that join them. The last ten leading research organizations, most recently a team of 35 researchers at IBM working on enterprise AI.

The best decision I made at IBM started in a breakroom. Someone was talking to us while managing three applications at once, never once losing the thread of either. That observation became IBM Sidekick, and Sidekick became a pattern the rest of the portfolio adopted. Research works when it changes what gets built. That is the only test I care about.

Case 02

One customer, and a product about to be cancelled

IBM Network Intelligence  ·  2024 to 2025  ·  Research lead and manager

The ask was to polish the interface. The problem was that nobody had asked what job the product was for.

An agentic AI product with one customer in Japan and a cancellation date approaching. The team wanted help iterating the digital experience and reviewing sales materials. I agreed to help, and then proposed we redefine the problem first. Eighteen candidate capabilities went in. Five came out, chosen by data rather than by the loudest voice in the room.

02  /  The through-line

A device, a screen, and the space between them.

My career keeps returning to the same shape. A connected glucose monitor at Express Scripts: pairing for patients, the digital experience around it, and a monitoring layer for the clinicians and benefit providers watching the data. Dell Data Protection: client, tablet, phone, and wearable pairing over BLE and NFC, with fingerprint, iris, and smartcard authentication spanning client, VM, server, and cloud. An AI health companion at Fyve Labs: hardware onboarding for an in-home listening device, the app the family uses, and the monitoring systems the data scientists use.

Hardware, software, and the ecosystem that joins them. The interesting problems have always lived in the joints.

The training underneath it is a PhD in Industrial and Organizational Psychology with a concentration in human factors, which started in human-systems research for the Air Force Research Laboratory and Navy programs at Dahlgren. That background is why I reach for measurement theory when a team is arguing about a roadmap, and why the Situational Awareness literature turned out to be the thing that moved an executive room at IBM.
